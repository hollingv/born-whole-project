# Organization: Intact America

Intact America is the largest organisation in the United States working
to end infant circumcision. Founded by Georganne Chapin, the organisation
works through public education, research, and advocacy to change how
America thinks about circumcision. Intact America is a 501(c)(3) non-profit
based in Tarrytown, New York.

No professional medical association in the United States or worldwide
recommends routine circumcision. People in Europe, Asia, and Latin America
— where 90% of men are intact — are often shocked to learn that American
hospitals routinely remove part of a baby boy's penis shortly after birth.
The US circumcision rate has fallen from over 85% in 1981 to fewer than
49% today, as more parents realise the procedure is unnecessary. Intact
America believes the rate of circumcision should be zero, absent a specific
and genuine medical indication.

Intact America's position on infant circumcision is grounded in four
bioethical principles. Autonomy: a child cannot legally consent to
unnecessary surgery, and proxy consent is only valid for procedures
necessary to save the child's life or health — routine circumcision does
not meet this threshold. Non-maleficence: circumcision causes needless harm
including pain, surgical risk, and the permanent removal of healthy,
functional tissue. Beneficence: routine circumcision does not remedy any
illness and exposes children to unnecessary risk and trauma. Justice: girls
are protected by federal and state law from any form of genital cutting
while boys receive no such protection — this is a violation of equal
protection that Intact America works to correct.

The foreskin is not extra skin. It is a complex anatomical structure with
important functions. It protects the glans from friction, maintains natural
lubrication, and contains highly specialised nerve endings that contribute
to sexual sensation. Circumcision permanently removes this tissue, leaving
a scar on the penis. All circumcisions result in some degree of loss of
function and sensitivity.

Circumcision is painful, and there are real risks. Both common sense and
medical research confirm that babies are sensitive to pain. Even when
anaesthesia is used, circumcision pain is not eliminated and its effects
can be long-lasting. Complications can and do occur, including infection,
abnormal bleeding, removal of too much skin, urinary problems, and in rare
cases death. Studies report that approximately 117 infant deaths occur per
year in the United States as a result of circumcision or its complications.

Intact America opposes the use of public and private health insurance
funds for medically unnecessary circumcision and calls on the medical
community to stop soliciting, performing, and being compensated for
surgery that is not in the patient's interest. The organisation also
opposes the promotion of circumcision as a method of preventing sexually
transmitted infections including HIV, calling this socially irresponsible
and dangerous given that intact individuals in Europe and elsewhere have
lower STI rates than circumcised Americans.
