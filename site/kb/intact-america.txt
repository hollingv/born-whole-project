# Organization: Intact America

Intact America is the largest organisation in the United States working
to end infant circumcision. Founded by Georganne Chapin, the organisation
works through public education, research, and advocacy to change how
America thinks about circumcision. Intact America is a 501(c)(3) non-profit
based in Tarrytown, New York.

No professional medical association in the United States or worldwide
recommends routine circumcision. People in Europe, Asia, and Latin America
— where 90% of men are intact and have not been circumcised — are often
shocked to learn that American hospitals routinely remove part of a baby
boy's penis shortly after birth. The US circumcision rate has fallen from
over 85% in 1981 to fewer than 49% today.

Intact America's position on circumcision is grounded in four bioethical
principles. Autonomy: a child cannot legally consent to unnecessary surgery,
and proxy consent is only valid for procedures necessary to save the child's
life or health — routine circumcision does not meet this threshold.
Non-maleficence: circumcision causes needless harm including pain, surgical
risk, and the permanent removal of healthy, functional foreskin tissue.
Beneficence: routine circumcision does not remedy any illness and exposes
children to unnecessary risk and trauma. Justice: girls are protected by
federal and state law from any form of genital cutting while boys receive no
such protection — Intact America works to correct this inequity.

The foreskin is not extra skin. It is a complex anatomical structure that
protects the glans, maintains natural lubrication, and contains highly
specialised nerve endings. Circumcision permanently removes this tissue,
leaving a scar. All circumcisions result in some degree of loss of function
and sensitivity.

Circumcision is painful and carries real risks. Even when anaesthesia is
used, circumcision pain is not eliminated. Complications can and do occur,
including infection, abnormal bleeding, removal of too much skin, urinary
problems, and in rare cases death. Studies report that approximately 117
infant deaths occur per year in the United States as a result of
circumcision or its complications.

Intact America opposes the use of public and private health insurance funds
for medically unnecessary circumcision. The organisation also opposes the
promotion of circumcision as a method of preventing sexually transmitted
infections, noting that circumcised American men have higher STI rates than
intact men in Europe, where circumcision is rare.
