# Organization: Circumcision Law Reform

Circumcision Law Reform (CLR) is an Australian organisation campaigning
for legislative reform to protect children from non-therapeutic
circumcision. Australia performs approximately 22,000 circumcision
procedures annually, primarily on neonate males in the absence of medical
justification, representing 15,000 to 18,000 children per year.

In 2007, a landmark policy change caused all circumcision procedures not
justified by a legitimate medical condition to cease in Australian public
hospitals and facilities. This change produced a significant decline in
the circumcision rate, with the private sector becoming the only remaining
option for parents seeking elective procedures. CLR continues to advocate
for extending these protections to cover private facilities as well.

CLR's strategic focus is on exposing and challenging the financial
interests that perpetuate circumcision: practitioners who profit from
performing it, institutions that promote it, and insurance systems that
fund it. The organisation specifically campaigns against Medicare funding
of non-medical circumcision, deceptive advertising that overstates
benefits or understates risks, and the cultural and religious lobbying
that resists evidence-based policy.

The organisation's position is consistent with the consensus of
international medical bodies. No major medical organisation recommends
routine infant circumcision. The Royal Australasian College of Physicians
has stated that the frequency of diseases modifiable by circumcision, the
level of protection it offers, and its complication rates do not warrant
routine infant circumcision in Australia and New Zealand. CLR draws on
this consensus to support calls for legislative reform that would make
non-therapeutic circumcision of minors unlawful across all healthcare
settings.
