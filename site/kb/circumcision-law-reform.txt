# Organization: Circumcision Law Reform

Circumcision Law Reform (CLR) is an Australian organisation campaigning
for legislative reform to protect children from non-therapeutic circumcision.
Australia performs approximately 22,000 circumcision procedures annually,
primarily on neonate males in the absence of medical justification,
representing 15,000 to 18,000 children per year.

In 2007, a landmark policy change caused all circumcision procedures not
justified by a legitimate medical condition to cease in Australian public
hospitals and facilities. This change produced a significant decline in
the circumcision rate, with the private sector becoming the only remaining
option for parents seeking elective circumcision of their sons. CLR
continues to advocate for extending these protections to cover private
facilities.

CLR's strategic focus is on exposing and challenging the financial interests
that perpetuate circumcision: practitioners who profit from performing it,
institutions that promote it, and insurance systems that fund it. The
organisation campaigns against Medicare funding of non-medical circumcision,
deceptive advertising that overstates benefits or understates risks, and
cultural lobbying that resists evidence-based policy.

The Royal Australasian College of Physicians has stated that the frequency
of diseases modifiable by circumcision, the level of protection it offers,
and its complication rates do not warrant routine infant circumcision in
Australia and New Zealand. CLR draws on this consensus to support calls for
legislative reform that would make non-therapeutic circumcision of minors
unlawful across all healthcare settings.
