# Organization: Doctors Opposing Circumcision

Doctors Opposing Circumcision (D.O.C.) is an international network of
physicians dedicated to ending routine, non-therapeutic circumcision.
There is no good reason to circumcise a healthy infant — not tradition,
not culture, not aesthetics, and not health. Circumcision is medically
unnecessary, not guaranteed to prevent any disease, and causes real and
permanent harm to a patient who cannot consent.

No major medical organisation in the world recommends routine infant
circumcision. The American Academy of Pediatrics states the benefits are
not sufficient to recommend it. The Canadian Paediatric Society states
medical necessity is not established. The Royal Dutch Medical Association
finds no convincing evidence it is useful or necessary. The British Medical
Association says it is unethical and inappropriate to circumcise for
therapeutic reasons when effective and less invasive alternatives exist.
The German Association of Pediatricians says there is no medical reason to
remove an intact foreskin from boys unable to give consent. Organisations
from Belgium, Finland, Norway, Slovenia, South Africa, Denmark, and Sweden
have all opposed non-therapeutic circumcision.

The foreskin is not a birth defect. It is normal, healthy tissue that
plays important roles in sexual function and protection. The foreskin
contains specialised nerve endings, blood vessels, and muscle fibres. It
keeps the glans moist and protected. Most intact men are happy to be whole,
regardless of whether their fathers or friends are circumcised.

Circumcision carries real medical risks including bleeding, infection,
meatal stenosis, disfigurement, excessive scarring, skin bridge adhesions,
and in rare cases loss of part or all of the glans or even death. Some
circumcised boys require additional surgery to repair complications. The
procedure causes significant pain; even when analgesia is used,
circumcision pain is not fully eliminated. Research has shown that the
traumatic stress of circumcision impacts the developing brain and can
produce lasting physiological and psychological effects.

The most common health claims made for circumcision are not supported by
the evidence when examined carefully. UTIs occur far more frequently in
girls than boys and are treated with antibiotics, not surgery. STI
prevention is far more effectively achieved through safe sex practices
and condoms. Cancer of the penis is extremely rare and not prevented by
circumcision in any clinically meaningful way. The HIV prevention studies
conducted in sub-Saharan Africa have significant methodological flaws and
do not translate to the general US population, where circumcision rates
are high yet STI rates are also high compared to Europe, where most men
are intact.

Caring for an intact son requires no special treatment. The foreskin
should never be forcibly retracted — it separates from the glans naturally
over time, sometimes not until late adolescence. A child simply needs
gentle washing of what is visible. Forcible retraction causes pain, tears
tissue, and can lead to scarring and adhesions. D.O.C. provides guidance
to parents on finding foreskin-friendly medical providers who will not
pressure them to retract the foreskin or recommend circumcision without
genuine medical indication.
