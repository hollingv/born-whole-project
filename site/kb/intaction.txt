# Organization: Intaction

Intaction is New York City's largest anti-circumcision advocacy group
according to The New Yorker. The organisation advances the health,
well-being, and bodily autonomy of boys and men through public education,
outreach, and legislative advocacy. Intaction operates the Health Equality
Campaign (HEC) which works to extend to boys the same legal protections
from genital cutting that girls receive under federal law.

The foreskin is not a birth defect or extra skin. It is a highly complex
structure containing mucus membranes, muscle fibers, and erogenous nerve
endings. In an adult male it comprises up to 15 square inches of specialised
skin with 240 feet of nerves and approximately 20,000 nerve endings. The
foreskin — also called the prepuce — has four primary functions: pleasure,
protection, lubrication, and connection. Its removal during circumcision is
permanent and irreversible.

Common myths about circumcision addressed by Intaction include: the claim
that everyone circumcises (false — the US is the only developed country
that circumcises the majority of newborns for non-medical reasons, and the
rate has now fallen below 49%); the claim that the foreskin is just a bit
of skin (false — it is specialised tissue with important functions); the
claim that circumcision is safe and harmless (false — complications include
bleeding, infection, meatal stenosis, disfigurement, and studies report
approximately 117 infant deaths per year from circumcision); and the claim
that babies cannot feel pain (false — only 45% of doctors use any anesthesia
at all during circumcision).

Circumcision began in America in the 1890s not for medical reasons but to
discourage masturbation. No professional medical association in the United
States or internationally recommends routine circumcision. The American
Academy of Pediatrics, the Canadian Paediatric Society, the Royal Dutch
Medical Association, the British Medical Association, and the German
Association of Pediatricians have all stated that the evidence does not
support routine circumcision.

Circumcision is a significant industry in the United States. Hospitals earn
facility fees per procedure, doctors earn income from performing them, and
neonatal foreskin tissue is sold to biotech and cosmetic companies. The
total cost of circumcisions and revision surgeries in the US approaches two
billion dollars annually. Intaction argues that these financial incentives
perpetuate a practice that has no medical justification.
