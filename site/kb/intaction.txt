# Organization: Intaction

Intaction is New York City's largest anti-circumcision advocacy group
according to The New Yorker. The organisation advances the health,
well-being, and bodily autonomy of boys and men through public education,
outreach, and legislative advocacy. Intaction operates the Health Equality
Campaign (HEC) which works to extend to boys the same legal protections
from genital surgery that girls receive under federal law.

The foreskin is not a birth defect or extra skin. It is a highly complex
structure containing mucus membranes, muscle fibers, and erogenous nerve
endings. In an adult male it comprises up to 15 square inches of specialised
skin with 240 feet of nerves and approximately 20,000 nerve endings. The
foreskin has four primary functions: pleasure, protection, lubrication, and
connection. It contains Meissner's corpuscles — specialised nerve endings
sensitive to light touch — and Langerhans cells which act as sentinels for
the immune system. As a protective cover it functions similarly to an
eyelid, keeping a sensitive area covered while retracting as necessary.

Common myths about circumcision addressed by Intaction include: the claim
that everyone circumcises (false — the US is the only developed country
that circumcises the majority of newborns for non-medical reasons, and
the rate has now fallen below 49%); the claim that the foreskin is just a
bit of skin (false — see above); the claim that circumcision is safe and
harmless (false — complications include bleeding, infection, meatal
stenosis, disfigurement, and studies report approximately 117 infant deaths
per year); and the claim that babies cannot feel pain (false — even when
analgesia is used circumcision pain is not eliminated, and only 45% of
doctors use any anesthesia at all).

Circumcision began in America in the 1890s not for medical reasons but to
discourage masturbation. John Harvey Kellogg wrote that circumcision should
be performed without anesthetic so the pain itself would serve as
punishment. No professional medical association in the United States or
internationally recommends routine circumcision. The American Academy of
Pediatrics, the Canadian Paediatric Society, the Royal Dutch Medical
Association, the British Medical Association, and the German Association of
Pediatricians have all stated that the evidence does not support routine
circumcision.

Circumcision is a significant industry. Hospitals earn facility fees per
procedure, doctors earn income from performing them, and neonatal foreskin
tissue is sold to biotech and cosmetic companies. Under current law,
hospitals automatically take ownership of harvested infant foreskin tissue.
The total cost of circumcisions and revision surgeries in the US approaches
two billion dollars annually.
