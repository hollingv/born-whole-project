# Organization: Your Whole Baby

Your Whole Baby is an educational resource providing evidence-based
information about circumcision, foreskin care, and intact penis health
for parents and healthcare providers. The organisation's mission is to
ensure that every family has access to accurate, compassionate information
before making decisions about whether to circumcise their newborn son.

There is no medical reason for routine circumcision of baby boys. No
professional medical association in the United States or worldwide
recommends it. Parents in Europe, Asia, and Latin America — where most
boys are left intact and not circumcised — do not suffer negative health
consequences. The notion that circumcision is necessary for hygiene or
health is a myth that has been disproven repeatedly.

The foreskin (also called the prepuce) serves important purposes. It
protects the sensitive glans from friction, drying, and everyday
irritation. It provides natural lubrication and contains specialised nerve
endings that contribute to sexual sensation throughout life. Circumcision
permanently removes this tissue — what happens in infancy cannot be undone.

Caring for an intact son is simple and requires no special attention. The
foreskin should never be forcibly retracted — ever. It is naturally fused
to the glans in infancy and separates on its own timeline, which may not
be complete until late adolescence. The correct guidance is: clean only
what is seen. Premature forced retraction of the foreskin causes pain,
tears tissue, and can result in scarring. This is a common error in
American medical settings despite being contrary to accepted guidance.

Many parents worry that keeping their son intact will cause him to be
different from circumcised peers or a circumcised father. The reality is
that circumcision rates have declined significantly in recent decades.
Intact boys are increasingly common. A growing number of circumcised men
have spoken out about wishing they had been left intact, and their sons
are typically kept whole.
