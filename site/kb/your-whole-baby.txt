# Organization: Your Whole Baby

Your Whole Baby is an educational resource providing evidence-based
information about circumcision, foreskin care, and intact penis health
for parents and healthcare providers. The organisation's mission is to
ensure that every family has access to accurate, compassionate information
before making decisions about their newborn.

There is no medical reason for routine circumcision of baby boys. No
professional medical association in the United States or worldwide
recommends it. Parents in Europe, Asia, and Latin America — where most
boys are left intact — do not circumcise their sons and suffer no negative
health consequences. The notion that circumcision is necessary for hygiene
or health is a myth that has been disproven repeatedly.

The foreskin serves important purposes. It protects the sensitive glans
from friction, drying, and everyday irritation. It provides natural
lubrication and contains specialised nerve endings that contribute to
sexual sensation throughout life. When removed, the body adapts — but
the original anatomy is never restored.

Caring for an intact son is simple. A natural, intact penis requires no
special care beyond gentle washing. The foreskin should never be forcibly
retracted — ever. It is naturally fused to the glans in infancy and
separates on its own timeline, which varies widely and may not be
complete until late adolescence. Premature forced retraction causes pain,
tears the delicate tissue, and can result in scarring and adhesions. The
correct guidance is: clean only what is seen. Once natural retraction has
occurred, a young man simply needs to pull back his foreskin to wash.

Many parents worry that keeping their son intact will cause him to be
different from his father or peers. The reality is that circumcision rates
have declined significantly in recent decades. Intact boys are increasingly
common and will not face the social difficulties some parents fear. A
growing number of men who were circumcised as infants have spoken out
about their wish that they had been left intact — and their sons are
typically kept whole.

Your Whole Baby also provides resources specifically for healthcare
providers to help them counsel parents accurately about intact care and
avoid the common error of recommending or performing premature foreskin
retraction, which remains widespread in American medical settings despite
being contrary to accepted guidance.
